# Procedural behavior: definition and distinction (frozen)

This document states the formal definition of *procedural behavior* used throughout STICKS
and distinguishes it from a TTP and from a MITRE Attack Flow node. The definition is
machine-checkable (`src/contracts/procedural_behavior.py`); `comparison.json` and
`comparison_table.md` are generated by applying that predicate, so the distinction is
demonstrated rather than asserted.

## Definition

A **procedural-behavior element** is an execution-ready adversary action carrying all six
components:

| Component | Meaning | Field in the STICKS concretized step |
|---|---|---|
| technique_identity | the ATT&CK technique realized | `technique_id` |
| execution_order | position in the campaign sequence | `sequence` |
| causal_preconditions | facts required before the step | `requires` |
| causal_postconditions | facts established after the step | `produces` |
| verification | a declared observable effect certifying success | `evidence_predicates` (non-empty) |
| execution_grounding | concrete laboratory bindings for parameters | `bindings` (non-empty) |

An element is procedural behavior iff every component is present.

## Distinction from adjacent representations

The comparison is stated narrowly, in terms of the two components STICKS requires that a
technique-and-flow representation does not, by itself, guarantee.

- **TTP (ATT&CK technique in STIX).** Carries technique identity. It is descriptive: on its
  own it does not provide order, bindings, or a success predicate.
- **MITRE Attack Flow.** Represents relationships and sequencing among adversary actions
  (order, and conditions/assets). STICKS additionally requires **bindings to an executable
  environment** and **evidence predicates** for validating the expected effect. These two
  properties are the focus of the comparison.

The rows in `comparison_table.md` model documented capabilities of each representation to
make the distinction concrete; they are not a claim about every profile a representation
could take. The table is regenerated from the predicate, so any change to the definition
changes the table.

## Why this matters for the contribution

Framing STICKS output as *procedural behavior* — rather than as "a TTP sequence" or "an
Attack Flow" — answers the reviewer's question "isn't this just Attack Flow?" with a
concrete, checkable distinction: executable environment grounding and effect verification.
